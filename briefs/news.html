Top 5 market-moving

  1. The PBOC announced a package of measures at the Financial Street Forum (金融街论坛). Governor Pan Gongsheng said the PBOC will:
    • set up, with the financial regulator and the CSRC, a liquidity support tool for large redemptions at non-bank institutions;
    • widen access to the standing lending facility;
    • study switching its main open-market operation to overnight;
    • phase out the benchmark deposit and lending rates;
    • push for links between the interbank and exchange bond markets.

This is liquidity and market-structure support relevant to banks, brokers and bond ETFs.

  1. China's Q3 growth came in at 4.5%, beating estimates (CNBC). Exports surged and offset weak consumer demand. 长江证券 put growth for the first three quarters at 4.6% and said exports contributed the most. Other figures:
    • Retail sales rose 1.1% in January–September and 1.1% in September.
    • Property investment fell 20.6%.
    • Industrial robot output rose 29.1%.
    • Domestic demand contributed more than 70% of growth.
  2. CSRC Chairman 吴清 set a stability-first tone (稳字当头). He said listed companies' first-half net profit rose 19.5%, and dividends plus buybacks reached 2.1 trillion yuan this year. He promised:
    • four new measures, including a fund industry monitoring centre and stricter penalties for illegal share sales;
    • support for broker mergers;
    • adding RMB share counters and REITs to Stock Connect.

The first active ETFs were approved on both the SSE and the SZSE (9 in Shenzhen).

  1. Asian chip stocks fell again. The KOSPI opened down 3.17%, with SK Hynix down more than 4%. The Philadelphia semiconductor index has fallen 8 days in a row, and CXMT shares dropped more than 6% despite a profit turnaround. On the other side, 海光信息's net profit for the first three quarters rose 45.12%.
  2. The PBOC and 10 other departments issued a guideline on financing three innovation hubs: Beijing (京津冀), Shanghai (长三角) and the Greater Bay Area. It promotes tech finance, the social-security tech fund, and cross-border settlement for software and computing-power firms. It also warns against blind, duplicate investment.
